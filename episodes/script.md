# Final narration

Visa isn't your lender. So how does it make money?

That logo on your credit card usually marks a payment network.

Your issuing bank generally lends the money and takes the lending risk.

When you tap, the merchant's payment provider connects to that network.

Visa or Mastercard routes the authorization request to your issuing bank.

The bank approves or declines. The network helps coordinate the transaction.

Think of a toll road, rather than a car lender.

Networks earn fees for services, including processing and cross-border payments.

But the merchant's total fee doesn't all go to the network.

Interchange generally goes to issuers. Acquirers and processors earn their share.

Networks still face risks, including settlement exposure. They're not risk-free.

The hidden business? Connecting the payment, rather than funding your purchase.
